# Development log

## 2026-10-18 — Session 1: environment, stack, Milestone 1 + prototype

**Environment.** Linux container with Node 22, Python 3.13, headless Chromium + Playwright, no GPU.
GitHub and naturalearthdata.com are blocked by the network policy; the npm registry and PyPI work.
Godot could not be downloaded, so it was ruled out (see ARCHITECTURE.md).

**Map data.** Natural Earth (public domain) obtained through npm packages:
`@geo-insight/data` (NE 10m admin-1, pre-split per country) and `@cublya/world-atlas`
(NE 50m admin-0). Five territories missing from the admin-1 package (South Sudan, Western
Sahara, Palestine, Åland, Siachen) are filled from admin-0.

**Pipeline decisions.**
- Raw admin-1 has wildly uneven granularity (Slovenia 193 units, UK 232, Russia 86), so units
  are merged per country toward a √area target and huge regions split → 2,358 provinces.
- `shapely.coverage_simplify` keeps shared borders identical, which lets the pipeline export
  shared border polylines; the renderer strokes country borders without runtime topology.
- 97 provinces had no land neighbour; straits (≤ 50 km) plus sea lanes connecting every
  landmass make the whole graph connected (verified by a test).
- Capital provinces are renamed after their capital city (e.g. the merged Paris region was
  originally named "Loiret").

**Bugs fixed during the session.**
- Merge loop never terminated for countries made only of islands (fixed with a frozen set).
- Seams between adjacent fills from anti-aliasing (hairline stroke in the fill colour).
- `.kv` panels rendered as run-on text (text nodes are not grid cells; wrapped in spans).
- AI pulled armies off nearly finished sieges (AI now finishes sieges unless invaded).
- Reloaded games diverged from the original after a few days: country caches and ledgers were
  recomputed on load, and the AI reads them. They are now saved, and a test checks that a
  reloaded game continues identically.
- AI declared ~12 wars per year in the modern world (incl. France vs Dominica over a strait);
  war chance and government aggression were reduced.

**Results.** 26 automated tests pass; Playwright end-to-end run (new game → play → recruit →
move → fast-forward → save → reload) passes with no console errors. Benchmarks in PERFORMANCE.md.

**Next.** Milestone 3 economy (resources, production, construction, trade), then diplomacy and
government (M4), technology and historical scenarios (M5). See FEATURES.md.

## 2026-10-18 — Session 2: Milestone 3, the economy

**What was built.** 14 goods and 15 building types as data files; resource deposits and starting
industry for all 196 countries; monthly production with input chains, national stockpiles, a
world market with prices, tariffs, embargoes and war closing trade routes; CPI/inflation,
unemployment, living standard, food-driven population growth, development growth; infrastructure
(efficiency and movement); a construction queue with slots; munitions and fuel supply for armies;
AI investment; Economy and Industry tabs, a province build menu, resources/industry/infrastructure
map modes, 24-month history charts and calculation tooltips. Save format v3 with a migration that
installs the scenario's economy into older saves. Rules: [ECONOMY.md](ECONOMY.md).

**Decisions.**
- Province GDP = calibrated services + industrial value added. The calibration makes every
  province start at the scenario's GDP, so Milestone 1's budgets and armies stay balanced and the
  economy only moves GDP from there.
- The starting economy is generated by a Node tool that imports the game's own demand and
  efficiency functions (one source of truth) and iterates until world output covers world need.
- Goods are national pools and trade is automatic; agreements and transport costs are deferred.

**Problems found and fixed.**
- First calibration capped industry in 95 countries and starved the world of food: poor countries'
  goods demand was too large relative to their GDP. Demand now scales closer to income, the cap is
  70% and exempts farms, and the builder corrects its own targets per good.
- Prices and CPI rose 20–60% in two years: world output was below need after rounding and caps.
  Fixed by the self-correcting builder; prices now stay within ±15% over 6 simulated years.
- The monthly tick took ~24 ms: the market looped over every exporter–importer pair and called the
  war lookup per pair. Replaced with a factorised formula over closed routes only, plus one-pass
  upkeep and army counts: ~4 ms.
- Saves grew to ~940 KB: per-country arrays now use Float32, CPI values are rounded at the source
  (so reloads stay exact), trade-partner data is derived instead of stored, and browser slots are
  gzip-compressed (~280 KB).
- The AI built steel mills "for consumer factories" it did not have; bottleneck logic now only
  applies once the country produces the good.

**Results.** 39 tests pass (13 new economy tests: calibration, production chain, construction,
slots, infrastructure, trade and embargo, war, tariffs, famine, prices, army supply, occupation,
AI investment); the determinism-after-reload test now covers stockpiles, buildings, prices and
CPI. Browser end-to-end test builds, embargoes and switches economic map modes with no console
errors. Low profile at 4× CPU throttle: 43 FPS.

## 2026-10-18 — Session 3: Milestone 4, diplomacy and government

**What was built.** Opinion, trust, reputation and influence with full breakdowns; six proposable
treaties (alliance, defensive pact, NAP, military access, guarantee, trade agreement) plus subjects
and reparations from peace treaties; defence and economic blocs with membership votes; calls to
arms; peace terms for reparations, subjugation and independence; separate peace; rivals and
claims. Eight government types with faction approval, support, legitimacy, elections, coups,
revolutions, five policy groups and reforms. A data-driven event engine with 17 domestic events.
AI diplomacy (rivals, treaties, embargoes, blocs, influence) and an AI war decision that needs a
motive. Government and Diplomacy tabs, a relations section on every country, declare-war preview,
event and separate-peace dialogs, and diplomacy/government map modes. Save format v4 with a
migration. Rules: [DIPLOMACY.md](DIPLOMACY.md), [GOVERNMENT.md](GOVERNMENT.md).

**Decisions.**
- Opinion is computed on demand from current facts plus a sparse list of decaying modifiers, so
  no 196×196 matrix is stored or saved and every value can explain itself.
- Treaties and blocs feed the existing relation matrix (allied, access, co-belligerent flags), so
  movement and war code only needed to read new flags.
- Every AI decision depends only on saved state; a derived trade-partner cache that the AI briefly
  used broke determinism after reload and was removed from AI logic.
- Government effects (`govt.fx`) are derived on load rather than saved.

**Problems found and fixed.**
- Approval and stability started too high (78 and 68): drivers were re-centred and the approval
  curve now has an explicit base and spread.
- The AI signed ~600 treaties in 3 years and NATO grew to 43 members: added per-type caps,
  stricter acceptance bases, a higher bloc vote threshold, and treaty seeking every other round.
  Distant small countries also asked great powers for defence pacts; a pact partner must now
  border the country or its rival.
- Small countries picked great powers as rivals because of a threat term; rivals now come from
  dislike and lost land.
- A subject could not declare independence (it counted as allied) and the war removed its own
  subject treaty; checks were reordered and peace-treaty obligations survive a declaration.
- Monthly tick reached 33 ms; spreading AI diplomacy and events across days, compiled event
  conditions and cheaper motive checks brought it to ~9 ms.
- In the browser, fast-forward at 4× throttle fell from 364 to 84 days per 5 s: each new treaty
  repainted the whole map. Only relation-coloured map modes repaint now (370 days).
- Relations key/value rows merged into one cell when bare text was passed to the grid.

**Results.** 63 tests pass (24 new: treaty validation and limits, subjects and independence,
joiners, NAP breaking, access, opinion and rivals, proposals and influence, peace terms, separate
peace, trade tariffs, bloc votes, AI turn processing, faction approval, elections, coups,
revolutions, reforms, event validation, conditions and effects, the v3 → v4 migration and
determinism after reload with diplomacy). The browser end-to-end test changes a policy, improves
relations, opens both new map modes and resolves an event dialog with no console errors. Low
profile at 4× CPU throttle: 38 FPS.

## 2026-10-18 — Session 4: Milestone 5, research, eras and historical scenarios

**Built.**
- **Technology**: a 119-technology tree in a data file. It has 13 eras, 13 fields and
  prerequisites, and is validated for cycles and unknown ids. Effects are normalised to the 2026
  world, and technologies gate units, buildings and goods.
- **Research**: a budget line, points from √(spending) × education, a diffusion discount from
  neighbours and world adoption, banking, a queue that adds prerequisites, and an AI chooser.
- **Eras**: the era is derived from known technologies and sets mobilisable manpower.
- **Units**: 11 units from levies to armour, with roles, munitions, fuel and siege factors, plus
  army modernisation.
- **Historical scenarios**: a reusable loader. `tools/build_history.mjs` turns region-rule
  definitions into scenarios, and the economy builder seeds technology-aware industry. Four
  scenarios: Height of Rome 117, The Great War 1914, The Gathering Storm 1936 and The Cold War
  1962.
- **Events**: the engine gained dates, scenario and country filters, once-only events, news,
  premises (`exists`, `atWarWith`, `warBetween`, `hasTech`, `ownsAny`, `fired`…), history effects
  (`war`, `annex`, `transfer`, `release`, `government`, `treaty`, `techs`, `trigger`…), chains and
  a Historical / Plausible / Off setting. 114 historical events and 15 world events.
- **UI**: a Research tab, a technology tree overlay, the era in the top bar and Nation tab, a
  Technology map mode, Modernise, recruit lists filtered by technology, and event settings on the
  New Game screen.
- **Saves**: format v5 with a migration and remapping of technologies by id.

Rules: [TECHNOLOGY.md](TECHNOLOGY.md), [SCENARIOS.md](SCENARIOS.md).

**Decisions.**
- Technology effects are ratios against the sum of all real technologies up to 2020. That keeps
  every Milestone 1–4 calibration valid, so the Modern World plays as before, and still makes a
  1914 economy roughly half as productive per building.
- Research points grow with the square root of spending. With linear points, large economies
  ran away; the square root gives about the historical pace. From 1914, leaders gained 6
  technologies in 20 years against 5 historical ones.
- All periods use the one admin-1 world map: one owner per province, with unowned land for
  regions without states. Province ids, the renderer and saves need nothing scenario-specific.
- Deposits are copied from the Modern World (geology does not change); industry is sized to each
  period's demand and technology.
- Scenario definitions and events were drafted by three parallel workers from fixed schemas and
  tag lists, then reviewed and run through the real builder and engine.

**Problems found and fixed.**
- Event effects that were not basic fell through into the province-development loop because a
  `break` was missing, which would have written NaN development. Fixed and covered by tests.
- Historical army sizes made 1962 budgets collapse (Soviet −11 B/month, China −12 B/month): the
  game's upkeep model is calibrated to modern GDP. Starting armies are now capped at a third of a
  typical revenue.
- The AI dropped scenario rivalries on day one (France stopped treating Germany as its rival).
  Scenario rivalries now come with a fading "Historic enmity" opinion modifier.
- Italy could not join the Entente after the Treaty of London, because it was still in the
  Triple Alliance bloc. `breakTreaties` now also leaves shared defence blocs, and the event uses
  it.
- Milestone 5 first cost +40% simulation time per day. Per-game event lists, rolling before
  conditions, a lazy event context and a cached newest-unit lookup brought that to +23%.

**Results.**
- **Tests**: 79 pass, 16 of them new. They cover technology validation, starting technologies and
  lags, unit, building and goods gating, research, the queue and banking, diffusion, era manpower,
  the v4 → v5 migration and remapping, determinism of a 1914 game, loading and running every
  scenario, borders, subjects, dormant states and starting wars, releasing a country, Sarajevo's
  chain firing on schedule and not when events are off, events skipping when their premise fails,
  history effects, and the shipped event files.
- **Browser end-to-end test**: it starts research, opens the technology tree and Technology map,
  then starts 1914 as Germany and runs it. There were no console errors.

**Not done (honest gaps).**
- There is no scenario editor or colonisation.
- Navy, air and space technologies have only general effects until Milestone 6.
- The war AI ends the Great War early.

## 2026-10-18 — Session 5: Milestone 6, navies, air forces, logistics and strategic capabilities

**Built.**
- **Sea zones**: `tools/build_seas.py` rasterises the oceans on a 0.5° grid and grows 99 named
  zones from seed points by sailing distance. It adds 200 links with 8 hand-listed straits and
  canals (chokepoint provinces; Suez from 1869, Panama from 1914), ports for 986 coastal
  provinces and the zone of each of the 444 straits and sea lanes. The grid is saved for map
  picking and the naval map mode.
- **Navies** (`naval.js`): 16 ship types in 6 roles. Fleets sail, dock and repair. Naval battles
  sink ships; submarines avoid battle unless detected. Sea control and blockades, commerce
  raiding that cuts each coastal country's trade factor, carriers, shore bombardment, and armies
  that embark, sail and land, losing transports in hostile waters. Enemy-held waters close
  straits and sea lanes to land armies.
- **Air** (`air.js`): 12 aircraft types in 4 roles. Wings sit at bases and have a range and a
  mission. Air superiority decides how much close air support reaches a land battle; strategic
  bombing is monthly, against interceptors.
- **Logistics** (`supply.js`): per-country supply maps (home land, allies, depots, sea supply),
  overcrowding, attrition, and effects on combat, organisation and reinforcement; depots for
  the player and the AI.
- **Strategic** (`strategic.js`): nuclear programmes, warheads and missiles; delivery by
  missile, missile submarine or bomber; interception; strikes with fallout and nuclear winter;
  launch on warning; deterrence in AI war decisions; an arms-control treaty. Also cyber
  operations, four satellite kinds with effects, anti-satellite weapons and debris, and
  (fictional) orbital stations and off-world settlements.
- **Data**: 31 new units (16 ships, 12 aircraft, 3 fictional land units, each with a `domain`)
  and 13 new technologies (4 fictional). 19 strategic events and 64 events for the two future
  scenarios. Starting forces for every scenario come from `tools/forces/*.csv` through
  `tools/build_forces.mjs`.
- **Scenarios**: Flashpoint 2035 and Orbital Age 2085, both speculative fiction, built with a new
  "base" mode of `tools/build_history.mjs` on top of the Modern World.
- **UI**: an Army/Navy/Air recruitment switch, fleet and air wing cards, right-click orders for
  fleets and wings, forces drawn on the map, supply bars, depot controls, a Strategic tab, a
  nuclear strike confirmation, and Naval and Supply map modes.
- **Saves**: format v6 with a migration from v5.

Rules: [NAVY_AIR.md](NAVY_AIR.md), [LOGISTICS.md](LOGISTICS.md), [STRATEGIC.md](STRATEGIC.md).

**Decisions.**
- Sea zones are derived from the coastline rather than drawn by hand, so they line up with the
  province map. The 0.5° grid is coarse, so narrow straits and canals are listed by hand with
  the provinces that command them.
- Armies need no transport ships. Sea control decides whether they can cross, which keeps the
  model simple and still makes navies matter for invasions.
- One ship or aircraft unit stands for a group (4 destroyers, 24 fighters) so real order-of-battle
  counts fit the game's unit scale. Starting forces are capped at 15% of a typical tax revenue,
  as land armies were capped in Milestone 5.
- The AI recruits in whichever domain (land, sea, air) is furthest below its target share of
  upkeep, then the role most below its weight in a fixed mix.
- Nuclear use by the AI is limited to survival: heavy occupation or a lost capital, with far
  lower odds against an enemy that can retaliate. Deterrence works through the war decision: the
  AI will not attack a nuclear power without a deterrent of its own.
- Starting arsenals keep the largest programme level they already meet, so they are not built
  up toward the next level. Only arms control dismantles warheads.
- Starting satellites are capped at 12 per kind (three times full coverage). Beyond full
  coverage extra satellites only add upkeep.
- Fictional technologies, units and scenarios are flagged in the data and labelled in docs and
  in game text. Fictional technologies are given at a start only in scenarios marked `fictional`.

**Problems found and fixed.**
- **History builder (Milestone 5)**: a variable named `cap`, the upkeep cap, shadowed the
  capital. The first army stack of every historical country was placed at a bogus province id
  (the upkeep number) instead of the capital. Fixed while factoring the army code out into
  `placeArmy`; the upkeep cap is now `limit`.
- **Token units**: tiny countries always got one line unit even when its upkeep was far beyond
  what they could pay. They now get none when one unit costs more than 1.5× their upkeep limit.
- **Very large air forces** (thousands of drones in 2085) became hundreds of small wings and slowed
  the daily air pass. The forces builder now packs each type into at most about 6 wings
  (wing size max(6, ⌈units ÷ 6⌉)).
- **Supply after reload**: supply maps were first kept in a cache outside the state. A reloaded
  game rebuilt them mid-period from changed facts and drifted from the running game. The maps
  are now quantised to bytes and saved in `state.supplyCache`.
- **Country caches on load**: `deserialize` rebuilt the country caches on every load, and the AI
  reads them, so a game reloaded mid-month diverged from the running one. Caches are now kept
  from the save and rebuilt only when a migration, a map extension or a technology remap makes
  them stale.
- **Save validation** stripped every army path node at or above the province count. Those nodes
  are sea zones, so armies at sea lost their route on reload. The save/load determinism test with
  armies at sea found it; validation now keeps zone nodes and checks them against the zone count.

**Results.**
- **Tests**: 118 pass, 39 of them new. They cover sea zones and ports, canals and chokepoints,
  fleet movement and docking, naval battles and peace, blockades, submarine raiding, amphibious
  movement and transport losses, close air support, air superiority, range, missions, strategic
  bombing and rebasing, supply (depth, corridors, attrition, depots, crowding), nuclear strikes,
  launch on warning, arms control, cyber odds, satellites, ASAT and debris, nuclear winter, AI
  deterrence, strategic events, determinism at sea and in the air, save/load mid-war with armies
  at sea, the v5 → v6 migration, save validation of forces, and the future scenarios.
- **Browser check**: `tests/browser/m6.mjs` covers the new interface in the Modern World and both
  future scenarios.
- **Performance**: about 2.4 ms per simulated day in the Modern World, against 1.46 ms for
  Milestone 5 in the same session. At 4× CPU throttle in the browser, map interaction stays at
  30 FPS or above, but maximum-speed simulation roughly halves (PERFORMANCE.md).

**Not done (honest gaps).**
- There are no transport ships or capacity, no naval mines and no aircraft attacking fleets.
- The AI plans no amphibious invasions.
- Supply has no goods cost.
- Wing experience is never gained, and warheads are not assigned to launchers.
- Simulation throughput in the browser at maximum speed is about half of Milestone 5's.
- Any wing with a bomb load counts as nuclear delivery (drones and ground-attack wings
  included), missiles have unlimited range and submarine tubes are never used up.
- In Orbital Age 2085 the arms control treaties cap arsenals at 1,550, so the largest powers
  dismantle warheads from the start (by design of the scenario's treaties; easy to change).

**Fixed after review.** The AI's last-resort nuclear strike now falls back to cities when no
enemy army stands in a strikeable province; the Strategic tab's missile defence figure no longer
counts the player's own hypersonic weapons against them; nuclear powers' AI builds ballistic
missile submarines; supply no longer spreads through unowned land; AI depot replacement refreshes
supply; Greenland in Flashpoint 2035 gets a technology lag like other countries; `air.minInfra`
is in balance.json; the forces builder clears stale station and colony counts.
