# Development log

## 2026-10-18 — Session 1: environment, stack, Milestone 1 + prototype

**Environment.** Linux container with Node 22, Python 3.13, headless Chromium + Playwright, no GPU.
GitHub and naturalearthdata.com are blocked by the network policy; the npm registry and PyPI work.
Godot could not be downloaded, so it was ruled out (see ARCHITECTURE.md).

**Map data.** Natural Earth (public domain) obtained through npm packages:
`@geo-insight/data` (NE 10m admin-1, pre-split per country) and `@cublya/world-atlas`
(NE 50m admin-0). Five territories missing from the admin-1 package (South Sudan, Western
Sahara, Palestine, Åland, Siachen) are filled from admin-0.

**Pipeline decisions.**
- Raw admin-1 has wildly uneven granularity (Slovenia 193 units, UK 232, Russia 86), so units
  are merged per country toward a √area target and huge regions split → 2,358 provinces.
- `shapely.coverage_simplify` keeps shared borders identical, which lets the pipeline export
  shared border polylines; the renderer strokes country borders without runtime topology.
- 97 provinces had no land neighbour; straits (≤ 50 km) plus sea lanes connecting every
  landmass make the whole graph connected (verified by a test).
- Capital provinces are renamed after their capital city (e.g. the merged Paris region was
  originally named "Loiret").

**Bugs fixed during the session.**
- Merge loop never terminated for countries made only of islands (fixed with a frozen set).
- Seams between adjacent fills from anti-aliasing (hairline stroke in the fill colour).
- `.kv` panels rendered as run-on text (text nodes are not grid cells; wrapped in spans).
- AI pulled armies off nearly finished sieges (AI now finishes sieges unless invaded).
- Reloaded games diverged from the original after a few days: country caches and ledgers were
  recomputed on load, and the AI reads them. They are now saved, and a test checks that a
  reloaded game continues identically.
- AI declared ~12 wars per year in the modern world (incl. France vs Dominica over a strait);
  war chance and government aggression were reduced.

**Results.** 26 automated tests pass; Playwright end-to-end run (new game → play → recruit →
move → fast-forward → save → reload) passes with no console errors. Benchmarks in PERFORMANCE.md.

**Next.** Milestone 3 economy (resources, production, construction, trade), then diplomacy and
government (M4), technology and historical scenarios (M5). See FEATURES.md.

## 2026-10-18 — Session 2: Milestone 3, the economy

**What was built.** 14 goods and 15 building types as data files; resource deposits and starting
industry for all 196 countries; monthly production with input chains, national stockpiles, a
world market with prices, tariffs, embargoes and war closing trade routes; CPI/inflation,
unemployment, living standard, food-driven population growth, development growth; infrastructure
(efficiency and movement); a construction queue with slots; munitions and fuel supply for armies;
AI investment; Economy and Industry tabs, a province build menu, resources/industry/infrastructure
map modes, 24-month history charts and calculation tooltips. Save format v3 with a migration that
installs the scenario's economy into older saves. Rules: [ECONOMY.md](ECONOMY.md).

**Decisions.**
- Province GDP = calibrated services + industrial value added. The calibration makes every
  province start at the scenario's GDP, so Milestone 1's budgets and armies stay balanced and the
  economy only moves GDP from there.
- The starting economy is generated by a Node tool that imports the game's own demand and
  efficiency functions (one source of truth) and iterates until world output covers world need.
- Goods are national pools and trade is automatic; agreements and transport costs are deferred.

**Problems found and fixed.**
- First calibration capped industry in 95 countries and starved the world of food: poor countries'
  goods demand was too large relative to their GDP. Demand now scales closer to income, the cap is
  70% and exempts farms, and the builder corrects its own targets per good.
- Prices and CPI rose 20–60% in two years: world output was below need after rounding and caps.
  Fixed by the self-correcting builder; prices now stay within ±15% over 6 simulated years.
- The monthly tick took ~24 ms: the market looped over every exporter–importer pair and called the
  war lookup per pair. Replaced with a factorised formula over closed routes only, plus one-pass
  upkeep and army counts: ~4 ms.
- Saves grew to ~940 KB: per-country arrays now use Float32, CPI values are rounded at the source
  (so reloads stay exact), trade-partner data is derived instead of stored, and browser slots are
  gzip-compressed (~280 KB).
- The AI built steel mills "for consumer factories" it did not have; bottleneck logic now only
  applies once the country produces the good.

**Results.** 39 tests pass (13 new economy tests: calibration, production chain, construction,
slots, infrastructure, trade and embargo, war, tariffs, famine, prices, army supply, occupation,
AI investment); the determinism-after-reload test now covers stockpiles, buildings, prices and
CPI. Browser end-to-end test builds, embargoes and switches economic map modes with no console
errors. Low profile at 4× CPU throttle: 43 FPS.

## 2026-10-18 — Session 3: Milestone 4, diplomacy and government

**What was built.** Opinion, trust, reputation and influence with full breakdowns; six proposable
treaties (alliance, defensive pact, NAP, military access, guarantee, trade agreement) plus subjects
and reparations from peace treaties; defence and economic blocs with membership votes; calls to
arms; peace terms for reparations, subjugation and independence; separate peace; rivals and
claims. Eight government types with faction approval, support, legitimacy, elections, coups,
revolutions, five policy groups and reforms. A data-driven event engine with 17 domestic events.
AI diplomacy (rivals, treaties, embargoes, blocs, influence) and an AI war decision that needs a
motive. Government and Diplomacy tabs, a relations section on every country, declare-war preview,
event and separate-peace dialogs, and diplomacy/government map modes. Save format v4 with a
migration. Rules: [DIPLOMACY.md](DIPLOMACY.md), [GOVERNMENT.md](GOVERNMENT.md).

**Decisions.**
- Opinion is computed on demand from current facts plus a sparse list of decaying modifiers, so
  no 196×196 matrix is stored or saved and every value can explain itself.
- Treaties and blocs feed the existing relation matrix (allied, access, co-belligerent flags), so
  movement and war code only needed to read new flags.
- Every AI decision depends only on saved state; a derived trade-partner cache that the AI briefly
  used broke determinism after reload and was removed from AI logic.
- Government effects (`govt.fx`) are derived on load rather than saved.

**Problems found and fixed.**
- Approval and stability started too high (78 and 68): drivers were re-centred and the approval
  curve now has an explicit base and spread.
- The AI signed ~600 treaties in 3 years and NATO grew to 43 members: added per-type caps,
  stricter acceptance bases, a higher bloc vote threshold, and treaty seeking every other round.
  Distant small countries also asked great powers for defence pacts; a pact partner must now
  border the country or its rival.
- Small countries picked great powers as rivals because of a threat term; rivals now come from
  dislike and lost land.
- A subject could not declare independence (it counted as allied) and the war removed its own
  subject treaty; checks were reordered and peace-treaty obligations survive a declaration.
- Monthly tick reached 33 ms; spreading AI diplomacy and events across days, compiled event
  conditions and cheaper motive checks brought it to ~9 ms.
- In the browser, fast-forward at 4× throttle fell from 364 to 84 days per 5 s: each new treaty
  repainted the whole map. Only relation-coloured map modes repaint now (370 days).
- Relations key/value rows merged into one cell when bare text was passed to the grid.

**Results.** 63 tests pass (24 new: treaty validation and limits, subjects and independence,
joiners, NAP breaking, access, opinion and rivals, proposals and influence, peace terms, separate
peace, trade tariffs, bloc votes, AI turn processing, faction approval, elections, coups,
revolutions, reforms, event validation, conditions and effects, the v3 → v4 migration and
determinism after reload with diplomacy). The browser end-to-end test changes a policy, improves
relations, opens both new map modes and resolves an event dialog with no console errors. Low
profile at 4× CPU throttle: 38 FPS.
